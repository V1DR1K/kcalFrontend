# Design System: ScaleGrams

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Bitacora cotidiana"**

ScaleGrams is a calm instrument for a moment that repeats several times a day. Its dark field belongs to the phone-at-the-table scene: light enough to read in a kitchen or after training, quiet enough that a logged meal never feels like a verdict. The interface gives measurements a physical place, then makes the next action obvious.

The system favors a clear working surface over fitness spectacle. Information becomes prominent through hierarchy, spacing and semantic color, not through decorative containers. The public landing speaks in the same language as the product: it lets a visitor inspect the journal before asking them to enter it.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Dark, low-glare surfaces with mint reserved for forward motion and completion.
- Dense but breathable daily records with tabular figures and explicit states.
- Rounded, tactile controls sized for touch without turning every element into a card.
- Calm motion that confirms a state change instead of competing with the data.

## Colors

The palette is a night field with navigational light: neutral layers establish reading depth; color states establish meaning.

### Primary
- **Measured Mint:** the active path, primary actions, completed progress and selected states.
- **Mint Ink:** text placed on the primary action; never use it as body text on dark surfaces.
- **Deep Mint:** contextual selected backgrounds and restrained success fields.

### Secondary
- **Reference Sky:** water, informational context and secondary numeric differentiation.
- **Plan Amber:** fat values and warning states that need attention without suggesting failure.

### Tertiary
- **Recovery Coral:** errors, destructive actions and irreversible confirmation states.

### Neutral
- **Night through Ridge:** the five tonal layers that define page background, surface, raised controls, borders and inactive tracks.
- **Mist and Sage:** primary reading text and secondary explanation. Sage never carries a primary instruction alone.

**The Signal Budget Rule.** Mint is for a decision, a current position or a completed step. A screen may contain many measurements, but only one forward action should read as mint-first.

### Token Roles
- **Primitives:** `--color-*`, `--space-*`, `--radius-*`, `--type-*`, `--duration-*` and `--ease-*` are the documented source values. Use them before creating a local literal.
- **Semantic surfaces:** `--surface-*`, `--scrim-*`, `--primary-*` and `--error-soft` preserve the intended compositing for working fields, temporary layers and feedback states.
- **Feedback signatures:** version status, ghost suggestions, swipe actions and copy confetti have named tokens because their exact colors communicate a distinct, existing state. They are not general-purpose accents.
- **Scanner reticle:** `--radius-scanner-frame` names the frame shape. Its 12px corner offsets and 4px bracket strokes remain explicit geometry, not spacing tokens.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Hanken Grotesk, with DM Sans as fallback.
**Body Font:** DM Sans, with a system sans fallback.

**Character:** Hanken Grotesk gives headings a compact, measured lift while DM Sans keeps numeric and instructional text direct during repeated daily use.

### Hierarchy
- **Display:** carries public statements and major page names. It is compact, balanced and never used for dense body copy.
- **Headline:** names a task, day or panel with a strong reading entry point.
- **Title:** identifies a meal, setting or dialog without competing with the value it contains.
- **Body:** explains an action or state in a readable measure of roughly 65 characters when space permits.
- **Label:** identifies a field, macro or helper value. It is compact and high-weight, never decorative all-caps by default.

**The Measurement Rule.** Nutritional values use tabular figures when alignment matters; a number should read as data before it reads as decoration.

## Layout

Desktop work uses a persistent navigation rail and a contained content column. Mobile collapses the rail into a bottom navigation and keeps core actions within the thumb zone. Spacing follows a four-pixel base scale; tight data groups use adjacent steps, while a task or section earns a visibly larger gap above it than below its heading.

The landing is not a generic feature grid. It opens with a legible daily ledger, then moves through the natural sequence of recording, reading and adjusting. Its public action always leads to account access, never an implied public registration flow.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is structural and low glare. Most operating surfaces use a tonal shift and a single quiet outline; raised dialogs and the landing ledger use a soft, downward shadow to separate a temporary or demonstrative layer from the field beneath it.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Raised:** used by dialogs, blocking actions and the public ledger when it must sit above the night field.

**The Calm Surface Rule.** Do not stack a prominent border, bright glow and heavy shadow on the same resting container. Depth has one job at a time.

## Shapes

Controls use gently curved corners. Panels are rounded enough to group a task without becoming pill-like; dialogs receive the largest radius because they interrupt the normal field. Pills are reserved for compact statuses, filters and small values.

## Components

### Buttons
- **Character:** direct and tactile.
- **Primary:** mint with mint ink, reserved for the action that advances a task.
- **Secondary:** raised neutral surface with a visible outline.
- **Hover / Focus:** lift only on intentional action, preserve a visible focus ring, and never communicate a state through color alone.

### Chips
- **Style:** compact neutral fields for filters and macro labels; selected chips use the primary signal.
- **State:** selection is visible through text, background and border treatment.

### Cards / Containers
- **Corner Style:** working panels are softly rounded; dialog corners are more generous.
- **Background:** tonal layers establish grouping before a border is introduced.
- **Internal Padding:** use the spacing scale rather than local one-off values.

### Inputs / Fields
- **Style:** a raised neutral surface with a compact label and reliable touch target.
- **Focus:** mint edge plus a soft mint focus field.
- **Error / Disabled:** coral explains a problem; disabled controls reduce emphasis without reducing legibility.

### Navigation
- **Style:** muted at rest, mint only for the current location. Desktop uses the rail; mobile uses the bottom bar and preserves accessible labels.

### Daily Ledger
- **Style:** the public demonstration of the app's core value. It is a sequential record of real meal moments, not a dashboard of decorative metrics.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** use semantic tokens from the foundation before introducing a new color, radius, shadow or timing value.
- **Do** make the current task and its next action visible within a single viewport on mobile.
- **Do** use mint for active, selected, completed or forward-moving states.
- **Do** keep public product claims limited to functions that exist in the application.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use color as the only indicator of validation, completion or selection.
- **Don't** turn every informational group into an equally styled card.
- **Don't** use bright mint as decorative background coverage or body copy.
- **Don't** offer public registration while ScaleGrams remains account-access only.
